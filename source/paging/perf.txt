Historical paging speedup over the x86_64 0.15.2 adapter

Speedup = x86_64 mean elapsed time / paging mean elapsed time.
Above 1x is faster; below 1x is slower. Each comparison uses the same
workload and worker-thread count. Worker threads are not verified
dedicated physical cores.

Workload	1 thread	2 threads	4 threads	8 threads
Mixed-cost map	2.295x	16.908x	37.830x	61.992x
Leaf-only map	2.280x	19.433x	46.862x	78.209x
Intermediate-table map	0.802x	0.955x	1.202x	1.440x
Map region	1.089x	2.940x	7.804x	14.940x
Unmap	0.956x	5.457x	22.957x	41.371x

Geometric mean	1.081x	4.483x	11.847x	20.095x

Overall geometric mean across all 44 cases: 5.829x.
Region operations affect 16 contiguous 4 KiB leaves; splitting turns a
2 MiB mapping into 512 4 KiB mappings.

Caveats:
- Historical measurements used per-page locks, not the current default
  global content lock.
- The x86_64 adapter serializes all operations, including reads, with
  one whole-table mutex. Its range operations loop over point APIs.
- Only software page-table operations are measured. No table is loaded
  into CR3, and real TLB invalidations/shootdowns are excluded.
- These point estimates do not establish statistical significance.

Source: saved Criterion after-sharing estimates.json files under
perf-results/<workload>/<adapter>/<thread-count-and-items>/after-sharing/
in the original session artifacts. Ratios use mean.point_estimate;
geometric means are calculated from unrounded ratios.
